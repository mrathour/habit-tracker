What the app reads from Notion

The app only ever reads from Notion. It never creates, changes, comments on or deletes anything in your workspace. What Notion allows it to reach depends on how you connect:

  • Connect to Notion (the usual way): Notion gives the app read-only access to the pages and databases you choose to share with it while connecting. Notion itself refuses any attempt to change them.
  • Internal integration token (pasted in the app's advanced settings): the app can reach only the pages and databases you share with that integration, with the capabilities you gave it in Notion. Giving it Read content only is recommended.
  • Personal access token (pasted in the app's advanced settings): Notion gives the app the same access as the person who created the token, to everything they can see, without any sharing step. Notion doesn't limit these tokens to reading, but the app still sends only read requests.

Whichever way you connect:

  • To let you pick your work log, the app lists the names of the databases it can reach. After that, it reads entries from the one database you picked and nothing else.
  • From that database it copies each entry's start and end times, its name, its link to a parent task, its logged minutes, and any extra columns you choose to include. It also records the name and icon of your workspace, so it can show which workspace is connected.

Where your data is kept

  • On your computer only. The copy of your work log and the app's settings are kept in a database file in your Windows user profile. They're never sent to me or to anyone else.
  • Your Notion access is saved in Windows Credential Manager on your computer. It's never written to a plain file, to the app's logs or to this website.
  • The app's log files, also on your computer, record what the app did (for example "synced 42 entries"). They never contain your Notion access.

What this website does, and what it stores

The app uses this website only at three moments:

  • When you connect. After you approve access in Notion, Notion sends a one-time code here. This site exchanges it with Notion for your access, encrypts the result so that only the copy of the app that started the connection can read it, and hands it straight back to that app on your computer.
  • When the app renews its access, which Notion requires from time to time.
  • When you disconnect, so Notion can cancel the app's access.

At each of these moments your access passes through this site in memory, for the length of that one request, and is then gone. This site has no database and keeps no accounts. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics or tracking, and shows no ads.

If you connect with a token you paste into the app's advanced settings instead, this website isn't involved at all. The app talks to Notion directly.

Technical information

This site runs on Cloudflare. Like any website, it necessarily sees your IP address and basic request details while handling a request.

  • Its own logs record only which page was requested, whether it worked, and how long it took. They never record your access, Notion's codes, the contents of requests, or web addresses with their parameters.
  • To protect the service from abuse, it counts requests per IP address over short periods, about a minute. Those counts aren't kept beyond that.
  • Cloudflare handles this traffic under its own privacy policy.

Sharing

I don't sell, rent or share your information with anyone. The only parties involved are Notion, which you connect to yourself, and Cloudflare, which runs this site.

Your choices

  • Disconnect in the app. It cancels the app's access with Notion and deletes it from your computer, then asks whether to keep or delete the local copy of your work log.
  • Remove access in Notion at any time, under Settings → Connections. The app then stops receiving data.
  • Uninstall the app and delete its folder, %APPDATA%\HabitTracker, to remove everything it kept.
